# What Does a Propeller Confidence Score Actually Mean?

## Final narration

If Propeller shows 72 confidence on a player prop, does that mean the pick has a 72 percent chance of hitting?

No. And the easiest way to understand the difference is to see exactly where confidence appears, what sits beside it, and what to inspect next.

In this walkthrough, we’ll use the same historical prop across the desktop and mobile platforms. The example on screen shows Luis Torrens, LESS than 0.5 RBIs, with 79 confidence. The number is different from 72, but it is read in exactly the same way.

Let’s start on the desktop Props Board.

Each row keeps the player, market, line, direction, source, and confidence together. Confidence helps you decide which props may be worth opening first. It is a research-priority signal—not a promise about the result.

Select a player to open the full Prop Detail page.

At the top, Propeller shows the player and market. In this example, the recommendation is LESS 0.5 RBIs. The direction is LESS. The line is 0.5. Those two pieces must always be read together.

On desktop, you can find the line beside the recommendation at the top of the page. It also appears in the recommendation card and again in the market panel on the right. Repeating the line matters because a move from 0.5 to 1.5 creates a different question.

On mobile, the same information is placed near the top of Pick Detail. LESS 0.5 appears beside the stat, and the Line Check card repeats this line while comparing it with the available market average.

Before using any score, confirm that the line on your platform still matches the line Propeller analyzed.

Now look at confidence.

On desktop, confidence appears in its own summary card. This historical example shows 79. On mobile, the same score is displayed in the confidence ring at the upper right and again inside Model Read.

A score of 72 would be interpreted the same way: the model sees a comparatively strong directional signal for the side displayed. It does not mean a 72 percent chance to hit, and it does not guarantee the outcome.

The mobile Model Read makes that distinction especially clear. Confidence and chance to hit are displayed as separate measurements. Confidence summarizes the strength and agreement of the model’s directional evidence. Chance to hit is a separate estimate. Do not substitute one for the other.

Next, review the evidence behind the recommendation.

On desktop, scroll to Why This Lean. This section turns the model output into readable reasons, such as recent usage, matchup context, performance against the line, or role-related information.

Below that is the Model Breakdown. These are the independent analysis agents that contribute to the final direction. Depending on the sport and market, you may see agents covering market information, recent hit rate, injuries, matchup, venue, usage, lineup status, and other relevant factors.

The value is not that every agent always agrees. The value is that you can see where the recommendation came from. Broad agreement is different from one strong signal surrounded by conflicting evidence.

The mobile platform presents the same idea in Model Read. It identifies the pick side, the top driver, the level of agreement, and the signal mix. Continue down the page to Agent Breakdown to inspect the contributing agents individually.

This is where confidence becomes useful. A stronger score means the available signals support the displayed direction more strongly. It does not remove uncertainty.

Lineups change. Roles change. Markets move. Players leave games early. Sports outcomes also contain real variance. That is why Propeller should begin your research, not end it.

Here is a practical workflow for new users.

First, scan the Props Board and open a prop you want to understand.

Second, confirm the direction and exact line. On this example, that means LESS 0.5 RBIs.

Third, find the confidence score, then keep it separate from any displayed chance-to-hit estimate.

Fourth, inspect Why This Lean, Model Read, and the agent breakdown. Look for the factors doing the work and whether the evidence broadly agrees.

Fifth, verify the current line and player context on the platform where you make your own decision. If the line has moved or the context has changed, the original analysis may no longer answer the same question.

And finally, decide whether the prop deserves more research—or whether to skip it. Skipping is always a valid outcome.

So, what does 72 confidence actually mean?

It means Propeller sees a comparatively strong directional signal for the displayed side. It is not a 72 percent win probability. It is not a guarantee. And it is most useful when you read it with the direction, the line, and the evidence behind the score.
