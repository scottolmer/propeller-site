Power Play versus Flex Play comes down to one basic tradeoff. A PrizePicks Power Play requires every active pick to be correct for its listed standard payout. A Flex Play has a lower top multiplier at the same pick count, but it can still return money when one or two picks are incorrect. That describes the structure, not which option anyone should choose.

PrizePicks calls these Player Pick lineups. Its current help page says a lineup normally contains two to six projections, with athletes from at least two different teams. Some limits are regional. Colorado, for example, requires at least three projections, and access, age requirements, eligible sports, and other rules vary by location.

As of August sixteenth, twenty twenty-six, PrizePicks lists these standard Power Play payouts: three times the entry for two correct picks, six times for three, ten times for four, twenty times for five, and thirty-seven point five times for six. Power is all correct at the active pick count.

Flex uses tiers. The current standard chart includes two-pick Flex at two times for two correct, or point five times for one correct. Three-pick Flex pays three times for three correct, or one times for two. Four-pick Flex pays six times for four correct, or one point five times for three. Five-pick and six-pick Flex add a third partial-result tier. The complete chart is on screen.

Here is a clean four-pick example using a twenty-dollar entry. At the published standard rate, a four-pick Power Play returns two hundred dollars if all four picks are correct. If one active pick is wrong, it does not earn that listed Power payout. A four-pick Flex returns one hundred twenty dollars for four correct, or thirty dollars for three correct. Those are total payouts, not profit, and this comparison is not an expected-value claim.

Now the most important limitation. PrizePicks says the payout on the individual details screen is disclosed before submission and can differ from the standard chart. Projection type, promotions, same-game or same-team combinations, combined Player and Team Picks, fees, and other pre-established factors can change the displayed amount. Always treat that current details screen as the controlling number.

DNPs, eligible Reboots, and ties can also change the outcome. A DNP or eligible Reboot normally removes that projection and reverts the lineup down one tier. A four-pick Power becomes a three-pick Power; a four-pick Flex becomes a three-pick Flex. A two-pick lineup that reverts is normally refunded, and a reverted lineup can become ineligible if every remaining athlete is from the same team. A tie also lowers the payout tier, but the tied projection is not removed for the minimum-pick or same-team rules. Sport-specific conditions still apply.

That output is a mathematical scenario, not a prediction, probability claim, recommendation, or promise about a real lineup.

So the short answer is simple. Power concentrates the listed payout on getting every active pick correct. Flex exchanges some of the top standard multiplier for additional payout tiers. Neither label tells you whether an entry is good, and the general chart never overrides the current details screen.
