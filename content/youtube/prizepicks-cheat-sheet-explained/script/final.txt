A useful PrizePicks cheat sheet is not a list of guaranteed picks. It is a dated research snapshot that tells you exactly who, what stat, which line, what direction the evidence supports, how strong that signal is, and what still needs to be checked.

Start with the identity of the question. You need the player, team, stat type, and exact number. A lean on more than eight and a half rebounds is not the same question as more than nine and a half. If the number changes, the research may no longer apply.

Next comes freshness. A trustworthy sheet should show when the analysis was produced and whether the underlying line is still available. A page title that says current is not enough. The row, its source, and its timestamp all have to agree.

That confidence score means the available signals strongly support the displayed direction. It is not a seventy-nine percent chance of winning, and it is not a guarantee.

Now inspect the supporting data. For this dated example, the analysis included recent hit-rate evidence, park context, usage, market context, and an injury check. The injury signal specifically said no injury report data was available. That missing data is part of the answer. A good cheat sheet shows limitations instead of hiding them.

The evidence also needs context. A recent trend can describe what happened, but it cannot promise what happens next. Park and matchup information may change the environment, but not the final outcome. Market-implied context is not true probability. And one strong signal should not erase late lineup news, role changes, weather, or a moved line.

Then separate direction from confidence. Direction answers which side the combined evidence supports. Confidence answers how strongly the model supports that side for the displayed line. Neither one tells you the payout, entry correlation, or whether PrizePicks currently offers that exact projection.

That last point is the required platform check. Propeller is independent and its public page is not a PrizePicks-native feed. At the time of this capture, Propeller's current analysis existed, but the platform-backed PrizePicks line endpoint returned no matching records. So this row cannot be presented as a current PrizePicks projection. The correct result is stop, not assume.

A cheat sheet cannot establish that the player is active, the line is unchanged, the projection is available in your location, the entry terms are favorable, or the outcome will hit. It also cannot replace the final screen inside PrizePicks.

So use this checklist. Confirm the player. Confirm the stat. Read the exact line. Check the timestamp. Inspect the supporting evidence and missing inputs. Read direction and confidence separately. Then verify the actual PrizePicks projection before making your own decision.

That is what belongs in a trustworthy cheat sheet: a clear research question, inspectable evidence, visible limits, and an honest line check. Propeller is a research and analytics platform, not a sportsbook, and no model signal guarantees an outcome.
