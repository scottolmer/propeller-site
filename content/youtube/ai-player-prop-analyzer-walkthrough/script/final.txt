If you are opening Propeller for the first time, here is the full player-prop research workflow in the order that matters.

Start with the exact question in front of you: the player, stat, direction, and current line. Those details stay together. A strong opinion about a player is not enough; the research only makes sense against the number you can actually use.

On desktop, choose a listed prop and open its detail view. First, find the line. Then confirm whether the analysis is leaning More or Less. On mobile, the same essentials appear at the top of the prop detail screen, so you can verify the player, market, and number before you read anything else.

Next, read confidence correctly. Propeller's confidence score summarizes the strength of the available directional evidence for the side shown. It is not a promise, and it is not the player's exact chance of hitting the prop. Use it to decide which analysis may be worth opening first—not as a reason to skip the research.

Now move to the reason behind the score. The agent breakdown lets you see which available signals support the lean, which signals conflict, and which one is doing the most work. Depending on the sport and market, that may include opportunity, matchup, injury context, pace or environment, recent performance, and market information.

Do not expect every agent to agree. A useful analysis can include a strong opportunity signal and a caution from the matchup or market. The point is to understand the evidence, not to turn every card into a vote. On mobile, open Model Read and Agent Breakdown for the same explanation in a more compact view.

As you read the cards, ask three simple questions. What is the strongest reason for the displayed side? Is that reason current, or is it based on an older trend? And is there a counterargument that changes how much weight you give the lean? A high score with one important unresolved concern deserves more review than a clean, broadly supported analysis. The breakdown makes that judgment visible instead of hiding it behind one number.

Recent form belongs in that same context. A player who cleared a number in several recent games may have had a different role, opponent, or line. Use the recent results to form a question, then use the agents and current information to test it. Do not treat a hit-rate streak as a forecast by itself.

Then check the context that can change. Confirm the displayed line is still the line you are considering. Look for late lineup or injury news, and make sure you are not treating a changed number as the same research question. If the evidence is missing, mixed, or stale, that is a valid reason to pass.

Passing is part of the workflow. You do not need to force a conclusion because a prop is available or because the confidence score caught your eye. If the line has moved, a key player is questionable, or the available agents point in different directions without a clear explanation, wait for better information or move on.

Here is the workflow to repeat: confirm the exact line, read the direction and confidence, inspect the agents, verify freshness and context, then decide whether the evidence is strong enough to research further. The goal is a clearer process—not an opinion on every prop.
