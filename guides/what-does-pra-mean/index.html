PRA meaning in basketball: points + rebounds + assists explained

By Scott Olmer, designated author and editorial owner. Published November 17, 2026.

Produced through an automated AI workflow. How our editorial process works.

PRA stands for points + rebounds + assists, added together into one number for a single player. A PRA prop line is one combined number compared with that single total, not three separate lines for points, rebounds and assists.

If you have been wondering what does PRA mean in basketball, that is the whole idea. This guide covers the PRA meaning in NBA stat lines using made-up numbers only, then shows what to check before you look at any line. What does PRA mean?

Every box score lists a player’s points, rebounds and assists. PRA in basketball simply adds those three counting stats for the same player in the same game. The letters come from the first letter of each stat: Points, Rebounds, Assists.

Nothing is weighted. A point counts as one, a rebound counts as one and an assist counts as one. Steals, blocks and turnovers are not part of PRA. When people ask about the PRA meaning NBA fans use, this plain sum is what they mean, though you should confirm the exact stat and game period on whatever screen you are reading.

How to add it up

Fictional example only. A made-up player finishes with 24 points, 5 rebounds and 5 assists. That is 24 + 5 + 5 = 34 PRA. Compare it with a fictional example line of 32.5: 34 is above 32.5. This is not a real player, a real line or a suggestion of any kind.

Notice what was compared: one total (34) against one line (32.5). The made-up player did not need to clear three separate numbers. A half-point line such as 32.5 can never equal a whole-number total, so the total is simply above or below it. Whole-number lines can tie, and tie handling depends on the platform’s rules.

Why it matters for PRA research

Because PRA blends three stats, the story behind the total matters more than the total itself. Useful things to look at include:

  • Minutes. More time on the floor usually means more chances to collect all three stats.
  • Role. Is the player a primary scorer, a rebounder, a ball handler, or a mix? Role changes can move the three pieces in different directions.
  • Pace. A faster game has more possessions, which can create more shots, rebounds and passes.
  • Matchup. The opponent’s style can change which of the three stats are available.
  • Injuries and rotation news. A teammate being out, or a coach changing the rotation, can shift minutes and responsibilities.

None of these guarantees an outcome. They are questions to ask, and some may be unknown until close to game time.

Where rebounds and assists come from

Rebounds and assists follow official NBA stat-keeping rules, and scorekeepers decide what counts as an assist or a rebound. The NBA.com stats glossary defines the terms in general. A platform may use its own data provider, so check how the platform you are reading settles its props.

Related combos: PR, PA, RA and stocks

PRA is one of several combined stat lines. The same adding idea applies to each:

  • PR is points + rebounds.
  • PA is points + assists.
  • RA is rebounds + assists.
  • Stocks is steals + blocks, the two defensive counting stats.

Each is its own stat, with its own line. A PR line is not a PRA line with something removed; compare each total only with the line for that exact stat.

A double-double is a different idea: it needs 10 or more in two separate stats, not one added-up total. See what is a double-double in basketball.

Checklist: before you look at any PRA line

  1. Confirm the stat. Is it PRA, or PR, PA, RA or a single stat?
  2. Confirm the player and the period. A full-game total belongs with a full-game line.
  3. Read the exact line. Copy the decimal, such as 32.5, and note the direction.
  4. Check minutes and role. What do you know about expected playing time?
  5. Check news. Look for injuries and rotation changes, and note what is still unknown.
  6. Read the platform’s rules. Overtime, did-not-play and ties are settled by the platform, not by this guide.

If something on the list is unclear, it is fine to wait for more information or skip the line.

Common questions about PRA

Does overtime count toward PRA?

For standard full-game player props, overtime stats typically count, but platforms differ. Check the named platform’s rules for the exact prop before assuming.

What happens if the player does not play?

It depends on the platform’s rules. Some void or adjust a prop when a player does not play, and others handle it differently. Read the rules for the platform you are looking at; this guide cannot settle that for you.

Is PRA the same as fantasy points?

No. PRA is a plain sum of three counting stats. Fantasy scoring usually weights categories differently and can include other stats, such as steals, blocks or turnovers, so a fantasy total will not match PRA.

Is a PRA prop the same as separate points, rebounds and assists lines?

No. A PRA line is one combined number compared with one combined total. Separate points, rebounds and assists lines are different questions with their own numbers.
